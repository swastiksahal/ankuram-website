In ISC (Classes 11–12), the academic jump is not cosmetic — it is structural. Maths becomes more formal (functions/relations language, proof-like reasoning in places), physics becomes numerical-heavy, and students must manage theory + project/practical components, not only written exams. ISC Maths: how the board paper is built

Class XI: paper structure + marks distribution

The ISC Mathematics syllabus states there are two papers:

  • Paper I: Theory (3 hours) — 80 marks
  • Paper II: Project Work — 20 marks

The unit-wise weightage for Class XI covers Sets & Functions, Algebra, Coordinate Geometry, Calculus, and Statistics & Probability. A detail most parents miss: the theory paper is divided into Section A (compulsory) and a choice between Section B or Section C (with different topic sets). That is why a generic "cover everything" plan is inefficient. You need to know which section your child will attempt and prepare accordingly.

Class XII: calculus dominates

For ISC Class XII Mathematics, the unit-wise marks distribution (80) includes Calculus at 35 marks — making it the single biggest chunk. So if your child is weak in differentiation/integration basics in Class XI, Class XII becomes a daily struggle.

ISC Physics: what the syllabus expects

The ISC Physics syllabus breaks assessment into multiple components:

  • Paper I (Theory): 70 marks (3 hours)
  • Paper II (Practical): 15 marks (3 hours)
  • Project Work: 10 marks
  • Practical File: 5 marks

Numerical focus is not optional. The syllabus explicitly notes that numerical problems are included from all topics except where excluded or where only qualitative treatment is required. This is why many students who were "fine" in Class 10 physics struggle: they are not trained to convert text to diagram to equation chain reliably.

Because ISC includes practical/project work, we also guide students on how to write clean observation tables and reports aligned to expectations stated in the syllabus.

ISC vs CBSE (Class 11-12) — what parents need to know

Parents often ask: "Is ISC harder than CBSE?" The better question is: what changes in the student's weekly workload? ISC Maths includes a formal project component (20 marks) in addition to theory. ISC Physics includes practical + project + practical file in a structured way. The ISC Maths syllabus emphasizes foundations like sets/functions and relations language early, and then leans heavily into calculus by Class XII. So a student who is not consistent weekly will feel "ISC is hard," even if they are capable.

For Classes 11 and 12, the choice between ISC and CBSE sets the stage for college. CBSE is deeply aligned with NCERT textbooks, making it the practical choice for students focused entirely on Indian competitive exams like JEE or NEET. ISC, however, offers incredible depth, especially in the sciences and English. The practicals often resemble college-level work, and the rigorous English curriculum is a huge advantage if you are applying to universities abroad.

We start with a diagnostic assessment to answer: Can the student manipulate algebra cleanly (factorisation, trig identities, coordinate geometry)? Are physics numericals failing because of concept gaps or because of math fluency? Our website lists a ₹750 diagnostic test for new students.

Maths: we teach to the marks distribution

If Calculus is 35 marks in Class XII, we build a plan where calculus is not treated like "one unit among many." We set weekly targets: differentiation skills leading to application (tangents/normals, monotonicity), integration techniques leading to application (areas, definite integrals), and revision loops so the same mistake does not repeat.

Physics: every numerical follows a repeatable template

We train a consistent method: 1) draw the diagram, 2) list known/unknown with units, 3) choose laws (not formulas randomly), 4) solve + check dimension/scale. This builds the reliable workflow ISC physics demands.

Project and practical guidance

ISC includes project and practical work across both Maths and Physics. We guide students on clean observation tables, structured reports, and presentation aligned to syllabus expectations.

Error tracking and revision loops

Most students repeat the same errors: sign mistakes, formula misuse, unit confusion, missing steps. We maintain an error log and re-test the same error types until the student's default habit changes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does ISC Maths have project work?

Yes. The ISC Maths syllabus specifies Paper I (Theory) and Paper II (Project Work) with 80 + 20 marks.

What is the biggest unit in ISC Class 12 Maths?

Calculus carries the largest unit weightage in Class XII (35 marks out of 80 in theory).

How is ISC Physics assessed besides theory?

The syllabus includes theory (70) plus practical (15), project work (10), and practical file (5).

Are numericals really required across the ISC Physics syllabus?

Yes — ISC Physics notes that numerical problems are included from all topics except where excluded or qualitative-only.

My child is strong in Maths but weak in Physics numericals — what usually causes that?

Often it is not "bad at physics." It is missing a consistent diagram-to-equation workflow and weak unit/dimension discipline, which ISC theory expects.

Can an ISC student do CBSE/JEE-style practice alongside boards?

Yes, but only after the board foundations are stable. ISC has its own distribution and project/practical commitments, so we schedule extra problem sets only when weekly consistency is in place.
