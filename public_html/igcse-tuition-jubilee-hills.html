Cambridge IGCSE Maths (0580): Core vs Extended

The Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics (0580) syllabus for 2025–2027 (which covers 2026 exams) states:

  • All candidates take two components
  • Core route: Paper 1 (Non-calculator) + Paper 3 (Calculator), each 1 hour 30 minutes, 80 marks, each weighted 50%
  • Extended route: Paper 2 (Non-calculator) + Paper 4 (Calculator), each 2 hours, 100 marks, each weighted 50%
  • Calculators are not allowed in Papers 1 and 2; a scientific calculator is used for Papers 3 and 4

How to decide Core vs Extended: This decision should be based on two things: 1) your child's current comfort with algebra/geometry speed, and 2) future pathway (especially if they may do AS/A Level maths later). Core is not "bad"; it is a pathway. Extended is appropriate when the student can handle multi-step algebra/geometry without freezing.

The 0580 syllabus shows that assessment objectives include both knowledge/understanding of techniques, and analysis/interpretation/communication mathematically. That is why "just do 20 sums a day" is not enough if the student's written steps are unclear.

Grades: A*–G vs 9–1

Cambridge explains that Cambridge IGCSE is graded A*–G, while 9–1 grading was introduced for GCSEs in England (with 9 being the top grade). In practice, some Cambridge syllabuses are offered in both grading scales depending on entry option; Cambridge also notes that grading-scale selection matters at entry time.

If you are comparing IGCSE results to CBSE/ICSE marks, do not try to convert by guessing percentages. Use grade thresholds and school guidance. The key thing to understand: an A* in IGCSE is not the same as "90%+" in the Indian system. Cambridge sets grade boundaries after each session based on paper difficulty and cohort performance.

IGCSE Core vs Extended — what parents need to know

The most important thing to understand about Cambridge IGCSE math is the grade ceiling on the Core tier. Core covers the basics, but the highest grade a student can achieve is a C (or 4). Extended covers everything in Core plus advanced algebra and trigonometry, allowing students to reach an A* (or 9). It is a tricky balance. Pushing a struggling student into Extended can be overwhelming, but keeping a strong student in Core limits their options for STEM degrees later.

Additional Mathematics (0606)

Additional Mathematics (0606) is often chosen by strong students who may move to A Level Maths later. Cambridge's syllabus (2025–2027) sets a clear, demanding assessment model:

  • Paper 1: 2 hours, 80 marks, non-calculator, 50%
  • Paper 2: 2 hours, 80 marks, calculator, 50%
  • Grades available are A* to E

This matters because many students assume Additional Maths is "more chapters." It is actually more of: functions language, algebraic manipulation under time pressure, and early calculus ideas. So if your child is entering 0606, tuition must be methodical, not rushed.

CBSE vs IGCSE — what parents need to know

Moving from CBSE to Cambridge IGCSE is a major shift in how students approach math. CBSE focuses on structured procedures and rapid calculation using standard NCERT textbooks. You learn a method and apply it. IGCSE is completely different — it tests how well students can apply math to unfamiliar, real-world problems. They do not just want the final answer; they want to see independent critical thinking.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between IGCSE Core and Extended Maths (0580)?

Core candidates take Papers 1 and 3 (80 marks each), Extended candidates take Papers 2 and 4 (100 marks each). Core is eligible for grades C–G, Extended for A*–E, and calculator rules differ by paper.

Are calculators allowed in IGCSE Maths?

Not in Papers 1 and 2 (non-calculator). A scientific calculator is used in Papers 3 and 4.

What grades does Cambridge IGCSE use — A*–G or 9–1?

Cambridge explains that Cambridge IGCSE is graded A*–G, while 9–1 grading was introduced for GCSEs in England (9 is the top grade).

What is Additional Mathematics (0606), and how is it assessed?

0606 has two papers: Paper 1 (non-calculator) and Paper 2 (calculator), each 2 hours and 80 marks, with grades A*–E.

Should my child take Additional Maths to prepare for A Levels?

It often helps, because it pushes algebra/functions and introduces higher-level techniques under timed conditions. The key is readiness — if core algebra is weak, the subject becomes stressful quickly.

Yes. Online can work well for IGCSE if the student is actively solving and getting corrections on written steps — because IGCSE marks depend heavily on method and communication.
