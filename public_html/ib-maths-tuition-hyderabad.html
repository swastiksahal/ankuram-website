AA and AI Are Different Mathematical Pathways

Both are rigorous, but they emphasise different relationships between theory, modelling, context and technology.

Analysis and Approaches places greater emphasis on algebraic manipulation, mathematical argument, functions and calculus developed through symbolic reasoning. Applications and Interpretation emphasises modelling, statistics, real-world data and thoughtful use of technology. The distinction is not “hard maths” versus “easy maths”; suitability depends on the student’s interests, university goals and way of working.

Standard Level and Higher Level also differ in depth, pace and expected independence. HL students face a larger conceptual load and more demanding connections, while SL still requires sustained understanding and careful exam communication. We teach the pathway actually chosen and help students see how the five syllabus areas connect rather than treating the course as a list of unrelated units.

GDC Fluency Must Include Judgement

Technology is part of IB Mathematics, but students must know what to enter, what to interpret and what to communicate.

Students practise graphing, solving, regression, distributions, numerical calculus and other functions relevant to their pathway. The focus is not on memorising button sequences in isolation. Each operation is connected to a mathematical question, domain, assumption and interpretation.

A calculator output does not replace reasoning. Students learn when a sketch, equation, substitution or statement is needed for method marks and how to report results with appropriate accuracy. They also estimate and use alternative checks so an input error does not become an unquestioned final answer.

Internal Assessment Support Without Taking Ownership Away

The exploration must remain the student’s work; good support develops questions, decisions and reflection.

We can help a student understand the assessment criteria, narrow an overly broad interest, plan suitable mathematics and evaluate whether a method is within the course. We discuss structure, notation, graphs, citations and the need to interpret results. The teacher does not supply a finished exploration or fabricate data.

Effective IA guidance asks questions: What is being investigated? Why is this model appropriate? What do the parameters mean? What limitation affects the conclusion? This keeps the student intellectually responsible and produces a more authentic exploration than cosmetic editing at the deadline.

Timeline management matters. Topic choice, initial mathematics, data, analysis, reflection and revision should not be compressed into one week. We help students create manageable checkpoints while respecting school feedback and academic integrity requirements.

IB Exam Technique Is Mathematical Communication

Students must combine understanding with command-term awareness, method evidence and time control.

Practice moves from concept questions to mixed, pathway-specific paper work. Students identify the given information, choose technology or hand methods intelligently and present reasoning in a form an examiner can follow. They learn to distinguish “find,” “show that,” “hence,” “interpret” and “justify.”

Timed sections and full papers are reviewed by error type. A lost mark might come from an algebraic gap, GDC entry, missing justification, inaccurate interpretation or poor time allocation. Revision then targets the pattern rather than returning indiscriminately to every chapter.

Formula-booklet familiarity is developed without turning it into a substitute for knowledge. Students practise locating useful relationships, recognising when a formula must be adapted and understanding the notation used. They also learn which essential algebraic steps and interpretations are not supplied by the booklet and therefore must come from their own reasoning.
