CBSE Class 10 Maths & Science: what's high-weightage in 2025–26

CBSE's Mathematics (Standard, Code 041) course structure for Class X breaks the 80 marks across these units:

  • Number Systems — 6 marks
  • Algebra — 20 marks
  • Coordinate Geometry — 6 marks
  • Geometry — 15 marks
  • Trigonometry — 12 marks
  • Mensuration — 10 marks
  • Statistics & Probability — 11 marks

What this means in practice: if Algebra is 20 marks, your child does not need "more tuition hours." They need clean basics (polynomials, linear/quadratic thinking) and speed + accuracy in standard board-style formats.

CBSE Science (Code 086) unit-wise marks (80):

  • Chemical Substances – Nature and Behaviour — 25 marks
  • World of Living — 25 marks
  • Natural Phenomena — 12 marks
  • Effects of Current — 13 marks
  • Natural Resources — 5 marks

In Chemistry, students remember reactions but lose marks in balancing, identifying reaction types, and explaining "why." In Biology, they can narrate life processes but struggle when CBSE phrases questions as "analyse similarities/differences." In Physics, they know formulas but misuse sign conventions, units, and diagram logic.

Mathematics paper pattern

The CBSE Sample Question Paper (2025–26) for Maths Standard shows:

  • 38 questions total across 5 sections (A–E)
  • Section A includes MCQs + assertion–reason questions
  • Section E includes case study-based questions
  • No overall choice, but internal choice exists in selected questions
  • No calculators allowed

That last point — no calculator — is exactly why many students lose marks in Trigonometry, Mensuration, and Statistics. They can do it slowly at home but not cleanly in 3 hours. CBSE's question paper design also allocates marks across cognitive levels: remember/understand, apply, and analyse/evaluate/create. So when parents say "my child solves NCERT, why are marks stuck at 60–65?", the missing link is often mixed practice (old + new chapters together), application formats (case-study/real-life interpretation), and step-marking discipline.

In CBSE Maths, the hard chapters are predictable

CBSE Class 10 is not random. Based on the unit-wise weighting, these clusters decide most outcomes:

Algebra (20 marks)

Algebra is the largest slice. The most common mark-loss patterns: factorisation habits are weak so quadratic equations become memory-based; students solve but don't write steps in a way that secures full marks; word problems are skipped because translation from English to equation is slow. In my CBSE batches, I start by testing algebra from Class 8–9 (identities, linear equations, factorisation speed) to determine whether the issue is concept or retention.

Geometry (15) + Trigonometry (12)

Together these are 27 marks. Students lose marks when they draw rough diagrams without labels or angles, know trig ratios but can't structure a proof or derivation question, and make sign errors without verifying reasonableness. My rule: every answer must be checkable in 10 seconds by the student after writing it. That's how you reduce silly mistakes.

Statistics & Probability (11 marks)

This is one of the most scoring units if students write the correct formula + substitution + final answer with rounding, and prepare clean tables for mean/median. We also train time budgeting, because Statistics can become a time sink if the student makes one early arithmetic mistake and compounds it.

CBSE vs ICSE — what parents need to know

Whenever parents ask me if CBSE or ICSE is "easier," I tell them it's the wrong question. They test completely different skills. CBSE math follows the NCERT framework, structured to prepare students for Indian competitive exams like JEE and NEET — it's all about speed and applying formulas to objective questions. ICSE demands detailed, step-by-step proofs and includes heavy internal assessments. If your child switches between these boards, they usually struggle not with their math ability, but with adapting to the new exam format.

Aspect CBSE Mathematics ICSE Mathematics
Syllabus Depth Streamlined, focusing on core concepts necessary for national entrance exams. Expansive, covering a wider range of topics with more theoretical depth.
Exam Pattern Leans toward structured, concise answers and MCQs. Requires detailed descriptive answers, step-by-step proofs, and heavy internal project weight.
Textbook Approach Strictly follows the NCERT framework. Uses diverse publications (like ML Aggarwal) that demand higher reading comprehension.
Scoring Dynamics Generally easier to score high if NCERT concepts are mastered. Strict marking scheme where presentation and steps matter immensely.

Bottom line for parents: Choose CBSE for a streamlined path to Indian competitive exams, or ICSE for rigorous, detailed analytical development. If your child is in ICSE, see our ICSE tuition in Hyderabad page.

CBSE vs State Board — what parents need to know

Choosing between CBSE and the Telangana State Board (TSBIE) is a big decision. CBSE is standardised across India, focusing on conceptual clarity and aligning with national exams like JEE and NEET. The Telangana State Board has a rigorous syllabus that covers a lot of depth, but teaching in many local schools often leans toward rote learning rather than practical application.

Aspect CBSE Mathematics Telangana State Board Mathematics
Syllabus Depth Standardised, conceptually driven, and highly integrated across grade levels. Detailed in raw content, but heavily dependent on localised textbook formats.
Resource Availability Massive, nationwide availability of high-quality mock papers and resources. Resources primarily limited to state-specific publications like Telugu Academy books.
Medium of Instruction Universally standardised in English and Hindi. Frequently incorporates regional languages, which aids local comprehension.
Career Implications Aligns seamlessly with premier national entrance exams (JEE, NEET). Ideal for students prioritising state-level universities and local entrance tests.

Bottom line for parents: CBSE offers wider national mobility, while State Board aligns well with state-level entrance exams like EAMCET. If your child is on the Telangana board, see our State Board tuition page.

Frequently Asked Questions

For CBSE Class 10, do you teach Maths Standard (041) or Basic (241)?

We teach CBSE Maths (including Standard). The paper structure and expectations differ, and we align practice to the current CBSE sample paper format (sections + question types).

My child finishes NCERT, but sample papers go badly. Why?

CBSE papers now mix MCQ + assertion–reason + case-study formats and reward application and multi-step reasoning. If practice is only chapter-wise, students struggle when topics are mixed under time pressure.

Which parts of CBSE Class 10 Maths are most important?

Unit-wise, Algebra (20), Geometry (15), Trigonometry (12), and Statistics/Probability (11) together form the biggest chunk of the 80 marks.

In CBSE Science, which units carry the most weight?

Chemical Substances and World of Living are 25 marks each (out of 80), making them the biggest scoring drivers.

Do you help with internal assessment?

Yes, but we're careful: the board paper is 80 marks, and internal assessment is a separate 20. Our priority is to make the student reliable in the 3-hour paper first, while advising on IA requirements where needed.

Is online tuition effective for CBSE Maths?

It can be, if the student is actively solving and getting correction on their written steps. CBSE rewards method and clarity, so passive watching doesn't help. Our live sessions with real-time written-work correction make the difference.
